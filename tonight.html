    How to use tonight’s data

    Stargazing really starts at nautical dusk, when the brightest stars and planets are easy to see, and gets best after astronomical darkness, when the Sun is 18° below the horizon and the sky is as dark as it will get. Faint galaxies and the Milky Way need that true darkness and a Moon that is either thin or below the horizon.

    Planets are bright and steady. If something brilliant and non-twinkling is low in the west after sunset, it is probably Venus; a bright, steady point high in the sky at midnight is often Jupiter. Use the chart’s time slider to see how the sky turns over the night — everything rises in the east and sets in the west, just like the Sun.
